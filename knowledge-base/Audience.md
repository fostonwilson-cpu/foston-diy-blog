# Audience

Who this site is written for. Read this before choosing a topic or angle.

## Primary reader

IT admins and help desk technicians at small-to-mid sized organizations, frequently a team of one to five covering support, security, and infrastructure at once. They are not specialists in any single domain — they're generalists who need to be right the first time because there's no second-line escalation.

## What they're doing when they land here

- Mid-incident, searching for the exact runbook step they half-remember.
- Building out their own SOP library and looking for a structure to copy.
- Onboarding a new hire and want something to hand them instead of re-explaining from scratch.

## What they need from a piece of content

- A structure they can scan in under 10 seconds and know whether it applies to their situation.
- Steps that assume competence but not memorization — no "obviously" or "just."
- Enough specificity to actually execute (named tools, named settings) without turning into a full vendor manual.

## What frustrates them

- Content that buries the fix under a defense of why the fix matters.
- Guides that assume a fully staffed team with dedicated security/network/helpdesk roles.
- Anything that reads like it was written to rank in search rather than to be used during an incident.
